Where is the New Year? Mapping the New Years' days of 2027

At midnight on January 1, 2027, the world will count down together. But for billions of people, that's not when the new year starts. Their year begins with the moon, the spring equinox, or a star cluster rising before dawn.

Warm-up (3 min): Press Strike midnight. Only some of these counters change. With a partner, decide why the others wait, and what that tells you about where calendars come from.

Investigate eight new years

Open each case file. The map flies to where the celebration began (its hearth), shades where it is observed today, and draws the documented paths of its spread. Read the case, study the map, then fill in your field notebook before comparing it with the reference notes.

Discuss with your partner

  1. Which marker moves the most between 2027 and 2038? Use the idea of a 354-day year to explain why.
  2. Lunar New Year and Rosh Hashanah also move, but they never leave their season. What do lunisolar calendars add to keep them there?
  3. Three of the solar new years begin near an equinox or solstice, or at the end of a rainy season. What does that suggest about the people who created them?

Synthesize: Free response

Use the map, the case files, and the calendar wheel as your stimulus. Answer in complete sentences. When you're finished, copy your work into your class's assignment.

Stimulus. In 2027, the Gregorian New Year falls on January 1, Lunar New Year on February 6, Nowruz on March 21, the Islamic New Year on June 6, and Rosh Hashanah at sundown on October 1. Between 2027 and 2038, the Islamic New Year moves from June to early February. The other dates stay within the same season.

Adapting for middle school

Assign four cases instead of eight (January 1, Lunar New Year, Nowruz, Islamic New Year). Use FRQ parts A–D only, and offer these sentence starters:

  • "This calendar follows the ___, so the date ___ each year."
  • "This spread by ___ diffusion because ___ moved/adopted it."
  • "In a place celebrating it, you might see ___."

Vocabulary: hearth, diffusion (relocation, contagious, hierarchical, stimulus), cultural landscape, diaspora, solar, lunar, lunisolar, equinox.

Notes on accuracy and sources

2027 dates were checked against the Royal Observatory Greenwich and Hong Kong Observatory (Lunar New Year), Hebcal (Rosh Hashanah), New Zealand's Matariki holiday schedule, Umm al-Qura tables (Islamic New Year), and published holiday calendars for Ethiopia and Iran. The March 2027 equinox occurs at 20:24 UTC on March 20, which is after midnight on March 21 in Tehran. The spread lines are teaching generalizations: each marks a documented route or destination, not a single event. Country shading is simplified, and many places have mixed or regional practices.

Technology

The map uses the ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript 4.34 with public Esri Living Atlas layers (World Countries Generalized, World Time Zones) and the light gray canvas basemap, so no ArcGIS login or API key is required. Schools that filter traffic need to allow js.arcgis.com, services.arcgis.com, basemaps.arcgis.com, and static.arcgis.com. Sounds are generated in the browser, so there are no audio files to load.
